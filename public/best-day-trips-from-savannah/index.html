Best Day Trips from Savannah

23.04.202419.04.2024
Home Travel Best Day Trips from Savannah

Savannah, with its historic architecture, lush tree-lined streets, and picturesque parks, serves as a perfect launchpad for a variety of day trips. This city, rich in both culture and history, offers more than just its immediate charms; it also provides easy access to several nearby attractions that are ideal for short excursions. These day trips from Savannah allow visitors and locals alike to explore the broader spectrum of experiences available in the region, adding depth to the traditional Savannah visit.

One of the most popular day trips from Savannah is to Tybee Island, just a 20-minute drive away. Known for its wide sandy beaches, Tybee Island is a straightforward choice for anyone looking to enjoy the sun and sea. The island not only boasts beautiful beaches but also the historic Tybee Lighthouse, which has been guiding sailors safely to shore since the 18th century. Visitors can climb to the top for panoramic views of the Atlantic Ocean and the surrounding landscapes.

Further afield, the city of Charleston in South Carolina offers a deeper dive into Southern history and is about a two-hour drive from Savannah. Charleston’s well-preserved architecture, historic plantations, and vibrant culinary scene make it a richly rewarding destination. The city’s cobblestone streets and horse-drawn carriages exude an old-world charm that parallels that of Savannah, but with a distinct character all its own.

For nature enthusiasts, the Okefenokee Swamp is an excellent day trip. Located approximately 90 miles southwest of Savannah, this expansive natural habitat is one of Georgia’s natural wonders. Visitors can take guided boat tours to explore the swamp’s waterways and observe its diverse wildlife, including alligators and numerous bird species. The Okefenokee offers a glimpse into a unique ecosystem unlike anywhere else in the state.

Another worthwhile day trip from Savannah is to Hilton Head Island, South Carolina. Just about an hour’s drive north, Hilton Head is renowned for its pristine beaches and world-class golf courses. The island also offers excellent biking trails, making it ideal for active travelers. Its coastal beauty and leisurely pace provide a refreshing contrast to the bustling energy of Savannah.

Historic Brunswick and the Golden Isles of Georgia, located about an hour south of Savannah, offer yet another flavor of the region. Brunswick’s charming downtown is lined with Victorian-era buildings, while the nearby islands — St. Simons Island, Sea Island, Jekyll Island, and Little St. Simons Island — each present their own unique allure. From the rich history of St. Simons Island to the millionaire cottages of Jekyll Island, the area is steeped in luxury and history.

Closer to Savannah, the town of Statesboro presents an opportunity to experience rural Georgia’s charm. Home to Georgia Southern University, Statesboro is buzzing with youthful energy and also hosts a vibrant arts scene. The town’s rural setting provides a backdrop for a quieter, down-to-earth day trip. Here, visitors can enjoy local breweries, historic sites, and the nearby Botanic Garden at Georgia Southern University.

These day trips from Savannah offer travelers a chance to explore the wider region and experience a variety of what the American South has to offer. From historic cities and idyllic islands to expansive natural reserves, the options are plentiful and diverse. Each destination has its own unique attributes but all are united by their proximity to Savannah, making them perfect for those looking to expand their exploration beyond the city’s historic squares and moss-draped oaks.

Exploring these destinations not only enriches a visit to Savannah but also enhances one’s appreciation for the broader cultural and natural landscapes of the area.

Tybee Island

Tybee Island, a small coastal city located just 18 miles east of Savannah, Georgia, is an ideal destination for day trips from Savannah, offering a blend of sandy beaches, historical sites, and oceanfront dining experiences. This quaint island can be reached in about 30 minutes by car from downtown Savannah, making it an easy getaway for a day of exploration and relaxation.

As one of the most accessible and popular day trips from Savannah, Tybee Island attracts visitors with its natural beauty and laid-back atmosphere. The island’s main draw is undoubtedly Tybee Island Beach, a vast stretch of sandy beach that is perfect for sunbathing, swimming, and various water sports. The gentle waves and wide shoreline make it a family-friendly destination where everyone can find something enjoyable to do, from building sandcastles to kite surfing.

Adjacent to the beach, Tybee Island Light Station and Museum stands as one of the oldest and most intact lighthouses in the U.S., having been guiding mariners safe entrance into the Savannah River for over 270 years. Visitors can climb the 178 steps to the top of the lighthouse for a panoramic view of the surrounding coastal landscape. The adjoining museum offers insights into the history of the area and the life of a lighthouse keeper, enriching the cultural experience of your day trip from Savannah.

Another historical gem that makes Tybee Island a worthy day trip from Savannah is Fort Pulaski National Monument. This well-preserved fort played a significant role during the Civil War and offers a glimpse into military history through its exhibits and live demonstrations. The fort’s massive brick walls and impressive cannons are set against the backdrop of the Savannah River, providing not only educational content but also picturesque views perfect for photography enthusiasts.

In addition to lounging on the beach and enjoying ocean views, visitors can engage in various beach activities that range from beach volleyball to jet skiing, making the most of the coastal setting. For a more structured outdoor activity, guided tours are available which explore the island’s ecology or its historical sites, providing both educational and recreational value to your day trip from Savannah.

With its combination of attractive beaches, historical landmarks, and enticing local cuisine, Tybee Island stands out as a top destination for those considering day trips from Savannah. Its proximity to the city, coupled with its variety of attractions and activities, ensures that visitors can enjoy a full day of exploration and relaxation without extensive travel. 

Hilton Head Island, South Carolina

Hilton Head Island, South Carolina, stands out as a premier destination for day trips from Savannah. The island is renowned for its pristine beaches, iconic Harbour Town Lighthouse, and world-class golf courses, making it an excellent choice for those looking to escape the city for a day. Located just about 45 miles northeast of Savannah, Hilton Head can be reached in under an hour by car, providing a straightforward and scenic drive that adds to the allure of a day trip.

One of the most appealing aspects of Hilton Head Island as one of the day trips from Savannah is its accessibility. Travelers can simply drive east on US-278, which is the main route connecting the island to the mainland. This direct route ensures that visitors can maximize their time on the island, whether it’s soaking up the sun on the sandy shores or exploring the various shops and restaurants.

Upon arriving at Hilton Head, visitors are greeted by the island’s stunning natural beauty. The beaches here are undoubtedly some of the most popular attractions, offering miles of soft sand and serene waters. Coligny Beach Park, for instance, is a favorite among both locals and tourists for its ample amenities and easy access. This beach is ideal for families looking to enjoy a day of swimming, sunbathing, and sandcastle building.

Another must-visit site on Hilton Head Island is the Harbour Town Lighthouse. This distinctive red and white striped lighthouse is not just a beacon for boats; it’s also a beloved landmark that offers panoramic views of the surrounding area. Guests can climb to the top of the lighthouse for a small fee, where they are rewarded with stunning vistas of the harbor, the nearby golf courses, and the vast Atlantic Ocean. The area around the lighthouse also features a variety of shops and eateries, making it a perfect spot for leisurely browsing and dining.

Golf enthusiasts considering day trips from Savannah will find Hilton Head Island to be a golfer’s paradise. The island boasts more than 20 championship golf courses, with some designed by golf legends such as Pete Dye and Jack Nicklaus. These well-maintained courses offer a range of challenges and scenic views, catering to both novice and experienced golfers looking for a day on the greens.

Beyond the beaches and golf courses, Hilton Head is also ideal for biking. The island features an extensive network of pathways that cover over 60 miles, providing a safe and scenic environment for cyclists of all skill levels. Renting a bike and exploring the island on two wheels is a popular activity that allows visitors to enjoy the beautiful landscapes at their own pace.

Shopping is another activity that should not be overlooked during day trips from Savannah to Hilton Head. The island is home to several outlet malls, such as Tanger Outlets, where shoppers can find great deals on everything from designer apparel to home goods. These outlets provide a diverse shopping experience, perfect for picking up souvenirs or updating one’s wardrobe.

Dining on Hilton Head Island offers a chance to indulge in fresh seafood, a staple of the local cuisine. Many restaurants feature dishes made with locally sourced shrimp, crab, and fish, served alongside other regional favorites. Dining al fresco at a waterfront restaurant provides not only a delicious meal but also a picturesque backdrop, enhancing the overall experience of a day trip from Savannah.

Jekyll Island

Jekyll Island, a serene destination ideal for day trips from Savannah, offers visitors an enchanting blend of natural beauty, history, and wildlife. Located about an hour and a half drive from Savannah, this barrier island is easily accessible and provides a perfect escape for those looking to immerse themselves in tranquility and adventure. 

When planning day trips from Savannah, Jekyll Island emerges as a top choice, mainly due to its rich history and picturesque landscapes. One of the island’s premier attractions is the Historic Jekyll Island Club Hotel. Once a private retreat for America’s wealthiest families, like the Rockefellers and the Morgans, today it stands as a window into the opulent lifestyles of the Gilded Age. The grand architecture and meticulously maintained grounds offer visitors a chance to step back in time. Historical tours available here are not only engaging but also provide insights into the fascinating past of this elite enclave.

Adjacent to historical indulgence, Jekyll Island also caters to nature enthusiasts. The Georgia Sea Turtle Center, a facility dedicated to the rehabilitation of sea turtles, allows visitors to learn about these marine creatures and the conservation efforts in place to protect them. This center is the only one of its kind in Georgia and plays a crucial role in educating the public about the impact of human activities on turtles and their habitats. Day trips from Savannah often include the center as a must-visit for anyone interested in wildlife and conservation.

Not far from the buzz of the conservation efforts, Driftwood Beach offers a striking contrast with its hauntingly beautiful landscape. This beach, known for its weathered and bleached driftwood, creates a surreal environment that feels more like a sculpture garden than a typical beach. It’s a favorite for photographers and couples looking for a romantic backdrop. The beach also serves as an excellent spot for picnics, where visitors from Savannah can relax under the sun, listen to the waves, and enjoy a quiet meal.

Day trips from Savannah to Jekyll Island are also perfect for those interested in learning more about the cultural and natural history of the area. The island’s historical tours delve into its transformation from a private club to a state park, highlighting significant events that shaped its current status. Moreover, these tours often explore other historical sites scattered across the island, providing a comprehensive understanding of its heritage.

St. Augustine, Florida

St. Augustine, Florida, stands out as a premier destination for day trips from Savannah, offering a unique blend of historical significance and charming attractions. Located approximately 175 miles south of Savannah, this ancient city can be reached in about three hours by car, making it an ideal spot for a memorable excursion.

St. Augustine holds the distinction of being the oldest city in the United States, established by Spanish explorers in 1565. This long history is vividly on display at the Castillo de San Marcos, the oldest masonry fort in the continental U.S. A visit to the Castillo is a must for day trips from Savannah, as it offers an impressive glimpse into the military strategies of the past and provides panoramic views of the Atlantic Coast.

Another beacon for those interested in both maritime history and breathtaking views is the St. Augustine Lighthouse. Climbing the 219 steps to the top rewards visitors with sweeping vistas of the coastline and the city. The lighthouse also features a museum that delves into the rich nautical history of the area, making it a worthwhile stop on your itinerary.

Day trips from Savannah to St. Augustine would not be complete without indulging in some of the city’s diverse activities. Sightseeing tours are particularly popular, with options ranging from trolley tours to walking tours that explore the city’s haunted past. These tours not only highlight the historical significance of St. Augustine but also bring to life the stories and legends that have been passed down through generations.

Ghost tours offer a thrilling twist on the traditional historical tour, perfect for those who enjoy a bit of supernatural flair with their history. These tours take visitors through ancient buildings and cemeteries, recounting tales of ghostly apparitions and unexplained occurrences that are sure to send a shiver down your spine.

Additionally, art enthusiasts will find a rich tapestry of cultural experiences in St. Augustine’s numerous art galleries and museums. The thriving art scene here showcases works ranging from contemporary to classical, with galleries often featuring works by local artists. The Lightner Museum is a particular highlight, housing collections of fine and decorative 19th-century art in a beautiful, historic building.

Day trips from Savannah often conclude with an exploration of St. Augustine’s various eateries and shops. The culinary scene here reflects a blend of its Spanish heritage and Southern charm, with plenty of seafood restaurants offering fresh catches and traditional Spanish dishes.

Beaufort, South Carolina

Beaufort, South Carolina, is an excellent choice for day trips from Savannah, offering a blend of rich history, natural beauty, and charming activities. Located just about 45 miles northeast of Savannah, it can easily be reached in under an hour by car. This proximity makes it a convenient and attractive destination for those looking to explore more of the South Carolina Lowcountry.

When planning a visit to Beaufort, a good starting point is the historic district. This area is a true showcase of antebellum architecture, with beautifully preserved homes and churches that date back to the 18th century. Walking tours are available that provide insights into the stories behind these historic buildings, making it a must-visit for history enthusiasts taking day trips from Savannah. The ambiance here is like stepping back in time, with streets lined with oak trees draped in Spanish moss.

Another top attraction near Beaufort is Hunting Island State Park. It is South Carolina’s most visited state park and offers an array of outdoor activities, making it a perfect spot for nature lovers. The park features five miles of pristine beaches, a historic lighthouse that visitors can climb, and several hiking trails that wind through maritime forests and salt marshes. 

The Beaufort History Museum is another highlight, especially for those who enjoy diving deep into the local heritage. Located in the historic Arsenal, the museum offers exhibits that detail the area’s rich past, from the Native American inhabitants through the Civil War and beyond. It provides a comprehensive overview that enriches the visitor’s understanding of the region, making it a worthwhile stop for day trips from Savannah.

For activities, kayaking is one of the most popular things to do in Beaufort. The surrounding waterways, including the Beaufort River, provide excellent opportunities for kayaking, where you can enjoy serene water trails and spot diverse wildlife. Several local outfitters offer rentals and guided tours, which cater to both beginners and experienced kayakers. This activity blends adventure with the tranquility of being on the water, ideal for day trips from Savannah looking for a mix of excitement and relaxation.

Antiquing is another favorite pastime in Beaufort. The town and its surroundings boast numerous antique shops and markets where visitors can find unique treasures. From vintage furniture and historic artifacts to retro decor and collectibles, antiquing here offers a tangible connection to the past and an enjoyable search for one-of-a-kind items. It’s a delightful way to spend an afternoon on day trips from Savannah, especially for those who appreciate the thrill of the hunt.

Sampling the local cuisine is an essential part of any visit to Beaufort. The town is known for its Southern cooking, with plenty of establishments serving Lowcountry specialties such as shrimp and grits, fried green tomatoes, and seafood fresh from the nearby waters. Many restaurants also feature picturesque views of the river, enhancing the dining experience. Enjoying the local cuisine is a highlight for food enthusiasts on day trips from Savannah, providing both a taste of the region’s flavors and its warm hospitality.

Overall, Beaufort, South Carolina, offers a rich tapestry of experiences, making it an ideal destination for day trips from Savannah.

Macon, Georgia

Macon, Georgia, is an excellent destination for day trips from Savannah, offering a blend of cultural, historical, and musical attractions. Located approximately 165 miles west of Savannah, the journey to Macon can be comfortably completed in about two and a half hours by car. This accessibility makes it feasible to plan a fulfilling day trip exploring Macon’s unique sites and activities.

Another must-visit destination for day trips from Savannah to Macon is The Allman Brothers Band Museum at the Big House. Situated in the house where members of the legendary rock band lived in the early 1970s, the museum showcases an extensive collection of memorabilia related to the band’s history and the southern rock genre that they popularized. Exploring this museum offers a nostalgic experience for music lovers and provides a glimpse into the lives of one of rock music’s most iconic bands.

Historic downtown Macon also stands out as a highlight on day trips from Savannah. The area boasts a collection of architectural marvels, including antebellum homes, historic churches, and beautifully restored buildings that line its streets. This part of Macon is perfect for strolling and enjoying the vibrant atmosphere, with numerous shops, cafes, and restaurants offering a taste of local cuisine and culture.

When it comes to recommended activities for day trips from Savannah to Macon, engaging in cultural and historical exploration ranks highly. Visitors can delve into the rich heritage of the region through guided tours, which often illuminate the intertwined stories of the people and the city over centuries. These tours not only enhance the understanding of Macon’s past but also highlight its contemporary cultural scene.

Music history tours are another engaging activity ideal for day trips from Savannah. Given Macon’s pivotal role in the history of southern rock and other music genres, these tours offer an in-depth look at the places that shaped musical legends. 

Dining and shopping in Macon also provide delightful experiences for visitors. The city’s culinary scene features a mix of traditional southern fare and modern gastronomy, ensuring that there is something to satisfy every palate. Shopping enthusiasts will find unique local boutiques and artisan shops offering everything from handmade crafts to high-end goods, making it easy to find a special souvenir to commemorate the trip.

Charleston, South Carolina

Charleston, South Carolina, is a vibrant city and a perfect destination for day trips from Savannah, offering a blend of history, culture, and scenic beauty. Located approximately 108 miles northeast of Savannah, it is easily accessible by road, making it an ideal choice for a quick getaway. The best routes to take from Savannah to Charleston are via US-17 N or I-95 N. Both routes offer scenic drives and take about two hours, making Charleston accessible for a leisurely day trip.

Day trips from Savannah to Charleston allow travelers to explore a variety of attractions and activities. One of the most compelling reasons to visit Charleston is its Historic Downtown. This area is the heart of the city, where cobblestone streets and antebellum architecture transport visitors back in time. A popular way to soak in the historical ambiance is through carriage tours of the historic district. These guided tours offer a unique perspective on the city’s rich history and architectural beauty, making them a must-do activity during day trips from Savannah.

Another significant attraction in Charleston is the Fort Sumter National Monument. Accessible by a short boat ride, Fort Sumter is a key site in American history, best known as the location where the first shots of the Civil War were fired. Visiting Fort Sumter provides a deep dive into the nation’s past and offers panoramic views of Charleston Harbor.

The South Carolina Aquarium, located on Charleston’s historic harbor, is another highlight. It’s home to a vast array of marine life and offers educational programs that appeal to both children and adults. The aquarium is a great spot to learn about the region’s aquatic life and conservation efforts, adding a learning dimension to day trips from Savannah.

Shopping enthusiasts will enjoy the Charleston City Market, one of the nation’s oldest public markets. Here, visitors can find a wide array of goods from local artisans, including handmade jewelry, art, and the famous sweetgrass baskets. Shopping at the Charleston City Market offers a taste of local culture and craftsmanship, making it a delightful experience for those on day trips from Savannah.

Moreover, Charleston’s historical significance and lush landscapes can be admired through visits to various plantations and gardens. These locations often feature beautifully preserved houses and gardens that tell stories of the city’s antebellum past. Exploring these plantations gives visitors a glimpse into the opulent lifestyle of the South Carolina elite, while the gardens offer a peaceful retreat from the bustling city.

Bluffton, South Carolina

Bluffton, South Carolina, is a charming coastal town, making it an ideal day trip from Savannah, Georgia. Located just 30 minutes from Savannah, this scenic destination offers visitors a unique blend of Southern hospitality and rich historical heritage, perfect for those looking to explore beyond the usual tourist spots.

Day trips from Savannah often lead visitors to the heart of Bluffton, where the Old Town area encapsulates the quaint essence of this historic town. A stroll through the Old Town not only provides a glimpse into the town’s antebellum past but also offers an array of boutique shops, charming cafes, and an inviting atmosphere. This historic district is the epitome of Southern charm with its well-preserved buildings and oak-lined streets, creating a picturesque setting for photographers and history buffs alike.

One of the main highlights in Bluffton is the Heyward House Historic Center. This center serves as a portal to the past, where visitors can delve into the local history through guided tours offered by knowledgeable docents. The house, built in the early 19th century, stands as one of the town’s few remaining antebellum homes. Exploring this historic site provides insight into the lifestyles of South Carolina’s coastal elite during the pre-Civil War era.

In addition to its historical and cultural attractions, Bluffton encourages outdoor activities that make the most of its stunning natural surroundings. Kayaking on the May River presents an excellent opportunity for visitors to engage with the local environment actively. The river’s calm waters are ideal for both beginners and seasoned kayakers, providing a peaceful yet invigorating experience. The journey along the river is complemented by the breathtaking views of the marshlands, teeming with wildlife and lush vegetation.

Art enthusiasts will find the local art galleries in Bluffton a treasure trove of creativity and talent. These galleries showcase works by local artists, ranging from traditional Southern art to contemporary pieces. A tour of these art spaces not only supports the local arts community but also offers a deeper understanding of the region’s cultural landscape. For visitors making day trips from Savannah, the galleries in Bluffton provide a refreshing contrast to Savannah’s own vibrant art scene.

Culinary experiences in Bluffton also reflect the town’s rich heritage, with an emphasis on seafood and Southern cuisine. Dining in Bluffton is a delight, with numerous restaurants serving up specialties that highlight local ingredients. Overall, Bluffton, South Carolina, serves as a perfect complement to Savannah, offering visitors a blend of history, culture, and natural beauty. Day trips from Savannah to Bluffton are not just about seeing a new place—they’re about experiencing the unique rhythms and flavors of the Lowcountry. This charming town invites travelers to slow down and savor each moment, whether through a leisurely walk in Old Town, a paddle down the May River, or a meal that tastes like Southern tradition. With its close proximity to Savannah, Bluffton stands out as a top choice for travelers looking to enrich their visit to the Southern coast.

Cumberland Island, Georgia

Cumberland Island, Georgia, stands out as a prime destination for day trips from Savannah, offering a unique blend of historical sites, natural beauty, and rare wildlife. Located just a few hours’ drive from Savannah, this remote barrier island is accessible via a ferry ride, making the journey there an adventure in itself. As the largest of Georgia’s Golden Isles, Cumberland Island invites visitors to explore its wide range of attractions—from wild horses roaming freely to the haunting ruins of Carnegie family mansions and vast, unspoiled maritime forests and beaches.

Travel logistics for day trips from Savannah to Cumberland Island are straightforward, focusing primarily on reaching St. Marys, Georgia, where the ferry to the island departs. Travelers can drive from Savannah to St. Marys in about two hours, which is a manageable distance for a day trip. The ferry ride from St. Marys to Cumberland Island takes approximately 45 minutes and is the only way to access the island, adding to its secluded and untouched appeal. It’s advisable to book ferry tickets in advance, especially during peak tourist seasons, to ensure availability.

Upon arrival, Cumberland Island reveals its spectacular natural and historical features. One of the most enchanting sights is the wild horses that roam the island. These horses, living in a feral state for generations, are often seen grazing in the dunes or along the marsh edges, providing iconic photo opportunities and a connection to the island’s wild spirit. The presence of these animals highlights the island’s role as a sanctuary for diverse wildlife, a key aspect that attracts numerous visitors on day trips from Savannah.

Another significant attraction on Cumberland Island is the ruins of the Carnegie family mansions. These remnants tell the story of a bygone era when the island was owned by the wealthy Carnegie family, who built several lavish estates there. The ruins, overtaken by nature and time, offer a hauntingly beautiful glimpse into the past and serve as a popular stop for guided historical tours. Exploring these ruins allows visitors to step back in time and imagine the island’s opulent past, making it a must-see for anyone interested in American history.

The natural environment of Cumberland Island is equally compelling, characterized by pristine maritime forests and expansive beaches. The untouched forests provide a dense canopy of live oaks, palmettos, and pines, under which a variety of wildlife thrives. These forests are ideal for hiking, with several trails winding through the lush greenery and offering peaceful solitude. The beaches on Cumberland Island are noted for their natural beauty, often described as some of the most beautiful in Georgia. With soft, powdery sand and clear waters, they are perfect for beachcombing and relaxing after a hike.

For visitors planning day trips from Savannah, a suggested itinerary on Cumberland Island would include starting with a ferry ride to the island, followed by a guided tour to gain both ecological and historical insights. These tours are invaluable for understanding the complex tapestry of the island’s ecology and history. After the guided tour, visitors can spend the afternoon hiking through the maritime forests and strolling along the beaches. This combination of activities encapsulates the diverse experiences Cumberland Island offers and makes for a fulfilling day trip from Savannah.

Statesboro, Georgia

If you’re considering day trips from Savannah, one compelling destination is Statesboro, Georgia. Located just about an hour’s drive northwest via US-80 W, this city offers a mix of educational, recreational, and leisure activities that can cater to various interests.

Statesboro is perhaps best known for housing the campus of Georgia Southern University, which itself is a significant attraction. A visit here can be more than just an educational tour; it’s a deep dive into both natural and cultural education. One of the university’s most enchanting spots is the Georgia Southern University Botanic Garden. This site is not just a collection of plants but an entire ecosystem managed to foster biodiversity and environmental education. It’s an ideal place for those who appreciate the serenity of nature and want to learn about the flora of the southeastern United States.

Another university-related attraction perfect for day trips from Savannah is the Center for Wildlife Education located on the same campus. This facility provides a unique opportunity to get close to a variety of wildlife, including birds of prey, reptiles, and a diverse array of other native species. It’s an interactive experience that educates visitors about wildlife conservation and the environment, making it a must-visit for families and wildlife enthusiasts alike.

Beyond the structured activities, Statesboro invites visitors to explore its local culture through its eateries and shops. The town offers a variety of Southern cuisine, from traditional barbecue to contemporary farm-to-table dining experiences. Shopping in Statesboro can be an adventure too, with numerous local boutiques and stores offering everything from handmade crafts to high-end fashion. This mix of dining and shopping options enriches day trips from Savannah, giving visitors a taste of local life and the opportunity to find unique souvenirs.

Day trips from Savannah to Statesboro are easily manageable due to the proximity and the variety of experiences available. 

Sapelo Island, Georgia

Sapelo Island, Georgia, offers a rich blend of cultural heritage and natural beauty, making it an ideal destination for day trips from Savannah. Located about 60 miles south of Savannah, this secluded barrier island is accessible only by a ferry from the mainland community of Meridian. Planning a trip here requires some coordination, as the ferry schedule is limited and reservations are necessary. Travelers should be prepared for a tranquil journey across the marshes, a perfect prelude to the island’s serene atmosphere.

Once on Sapelo Island, visitors are greeted by the expansive views and lush landscapes that characterize coastal Georgia. The island’s primary attractions contribute significantly to its allure, drawing visitors interested in history, nature, and relaxation alike.

The Reynolds Mansion is one of these key attractions. Originally built in the 1800s and later refurbished, the mansion offers a glimpse into the luxurious life once lived here. It’s a sought-after site for history buffs and those interested in architecture. Tours of the mansion provide insights into the lives of its various owners, from Thomas Spalding to automotive tycoon Howard Coffin, and later, tobacco heir R.J. Reynolds.

Another historic site is the Sapelo Island Lighthouse. Established in 1820 and meticulously restored in the 1990s, it stands as one of the oldest lighthouses in the United States. Visitors can explore the area around the lighthouse, learning about its role in maritime navigation and enjoying panoramic views of the Atlantic Ocean. For photography enthusiasts and history lovers, the lighthouse offers ample opportunities for both education and scenic captures.

Activities on Sapelo Island cater to a range of interests. Guided cultural and ecological tours are popular and provide a deeper understanding of the island’s ecology and the Gullah-Geechee community that has preserved its African cultural heritage over generations. These tours often include visits to the historic Hog Hammock community, a rare remaining example of a traditional Gullah-Geechee community.

Birdwatching and nature walks are other popular activities on Sapelo Island. The island’s diverse ecosystems—from salt marshes to dunes to maritime forests—are home to an array of wildlife, particularly birds. 

For many, the ultimate attraction of Sapelo Island is its capacity for providing a peaceful escape. The secluded beaches here offer a respite from the hustle and bustle of daily life. Day trips from Savannah to Sapelo Island allow visitors to unwind on these tranquil shores, where the rhythms of nature dictate the pace.

Each of these destinations offers a unique glimpse into the culture, history, and natural beauty surrounding Savannah, making them perfect for day trips that enrich any visit to the region.

Conclusion

Savannah, Georgia, renowned for its charming squares, historic homes, and weeping willows, serves as a gateway to myriad adventures that beckon beyond its city limits. Day trips from Savannah offer a diverse array of experiences, highlighting the natural beauty, cultural richness, and historical depth of the surrounding region. These excursions present opportunities to explore coastal landscapes, delve into the history of the South, and discover unique towns and attractions all within a day’s reach.

Day trips from Savannah can transport you to the serene beaches of Tybee Island, known as Savannah’s beach. This nearby barrier island, only a short drive away, provides a perfect sandy getaway where visitors can enjoy sunbathing, swimming, and the famous Tybee Island Light Station. Another day trip destination is the historic city of Charleston, South Carolina. About two hours north of Savannah, Charleston offers a preserved architectural landscape, vibrant culinary scene, and rich Revolutionary and Civil War history that parallels, yet contrasts with Savannah’s own storied past.

Exploring further, Cumberland Island presents another excellent option for day trips from Savannah. Accessible by ferry, Cumberland Island is Georgia’s largest and southernmost barrier island, offering pristine maritime forests, wild beaches, and wild horses roaming freely. It’s an ideal spot for hiking, wildlife viewing, and photography.

Just a short drive from Savannah, the charming town of Beaufort, South Carolina, offers a taste of Southern life. This picturesque coastal town, with its well-preserved Antebellum architecture, offers a quieter yet equally enriching experience compared to the more bustling Charleston. Visitors can enjoy leisurely carriage rides, waterfront dining, and visiting local galleries.

Not to be overlooked, the historic Fort Pulaski, situated between Savannah and Tybee Island, is a monument to the ingenuity of military engineering and the shifts in warfare during the Civil War. This national monument offers daily cannon firings, guided tours, and scenic trails.

Each of these day trips from Savannah uncovers different facets of the Southeast’s charm and character. 

Encouraging visitors and locals alike to venture beyond the city’s limits, day trips from Savannah not only enhance your appreciation of the region but also enrich your understanding of the complex tapestry that is the American South. These trips promise memorable experiences that highlight why Savannah is not just a city to visit, but a hub from which to explore the greater riches of Georgia and beyond.
